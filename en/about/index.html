About

The Agrippane story

Agrippane makes everlasting flowers from chenille (pipe cleaner), one bloom at a time. It begins with a craft that brings even a Roman bust of Agrippa back to life in flowers.

The story behind the name

The name Agrippane comes from the Roman general Agrippa. Reshaping even a hard marble bust into soft chenille flowers — that contrast is the symbol of Agrippane. In Korean, the ending “-ne (네)” evokes “Agrippa’s home,” a warmth that stays.

Why flowers that never wilt

Fresh flowers are beautiful but fade within days. Agrippane’s chenille flowers need no water and last. We believe giving a gift of flowers is a promise to keep that feeling for a long time — which is why “never wilts” sits at the center of the brand.

How it’s made

Every piece is handmade, bending chenille stems into petals, a calyx, and a stem, one at a time. Colors and arrangements are made to order, so even the same carnation becomes a different flower for each person. To learn more about the craft, see “What is a chenille flower?”.

What we make

From Parents’ Day carnations and coming-of-age roses to graduation and birthday bouquets, military service, recitals, conferences, openings, and proposals — we make flowers to prepare ahead for date-driven occasions. See them all in the occasion collection.
